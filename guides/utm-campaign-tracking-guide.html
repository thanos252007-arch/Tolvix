UTM Parameters Explained: Build Campaign Tracking Links

UTM parameters are small query-string labels added to a destination URL. Analytics tools can use them to group visits from newsletters, social posts, partner links and paid campaigns. They do not create traffic or prove that a campaign caused a conversion; they help describe the link that brought a visit.

The five common parameters

utm_source identifies the referrer or platform, such as newsletter or linkedin. utm_medium describes the channel type, such as email, social or cpc. utm_campaign groups links under a named promotion, such as winter_launch. utm_term is commonly used for paid-search keyword context. utm_content can distinguish creative variants or placements, such as header_button and footer_link.

Choose a naming convention before launching

Use lowercase names, hyphens or underscores consistently, and avoid switching between values such as Email, email and e-mail. Decide whether source means the platform or a specific partner and document that choice. Keep campaign names readable and stable. Avoid personal data, email addresses or confidential identifiers in query parameters because URLs can appear in browser history, logs and referrer data.

Build and QA the link

Start with the exact public landing-page URL. Add only the parameters you need, then open the final URL in a private browser window and confirm that it reaches the intended page. Check that redirects preserve the query string and that the landing page works with parameters attached. Use separate tagged links for distinct external placements, not for ordinary internal navigation.

Common mistakes

Do not tag links between pages on your own site as new campaigns; this can overwrite the original acquisition context. Do not use inconsistent naming or include secrets in a URL. Do not assume every analytics product attributes visits in exactly the same way. Review the reports and conversion setup in your own analytics platform.
