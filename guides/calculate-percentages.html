How to Calculate Percentages: Formulas and Worked Examples

Three common percentage questions use different formulas: a percentage of a value, a part as a percentage of a whole and percentage change.

← Back to Tolvix tools

Percentages express a value as a part of 100. They are used for discounts, marks, price changes, GST calculations, interest comparisons and everyday measurements. This guide explains the most common formulas and shows how to use the Tolvix Percentage Calculator.

1. Find a percentage of a number

Part = Percentage ÷ 100 × Whole

Example: 18% of 1,500 = 18 ÷ 100 × 1,500 = 270. If the whole is ₹1,500, the answer is ₹270.

2. Find what percentage one number is of another

Percentage = Part ÷ Whole × 100

Example: 45 is what percentage of 180? 45 ÷ 180 × 100 = 25%. The whole must not be zero.

3. Calculate percentage increase or decrease

Percentage change = (New value − Original value) ÷ Original value × 100

Example: a value rises from 80 to 100. The difference is 20; 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25% increase. If a value falls from 100 to 80, the change is −20%, or a 20% decrease.

4. Calculate a discount and sale price

For a ₹2,000 item with a 15% discount, savings = ₹2,000 × 15 ÷ 100 = ₹300. Sale price = ₹2,000 − ₹300 = ₹1,700, before any applicable taxes, delivery or fees. Try the Discount Calculator for a sale-price calculation.

5. Percentage points versus percent change

If an interest rate changes from 10% to 15%, it increases by 5 percentage points. Relative to the original 10% rate, that is a 50% increase. These describe different things, so use the wording that matches the question.

Common mistakes

  • Using the new value instead of the original value as the base for percentage change.
  • Confusing a percentage-point difference with a relative percentage change.
  • Rounding intermediate calculations too early.
  • Trying to calculate percentage change from an original value of zero; the usual formula is undefined in that case.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate 20% of 500?

500 × 20 ÷ 100 = 100.

How do I find the percentage of marks?

Divide marks obtained by total possible marks and multiply by 100, provided the marks use the same scale.

Is a 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease back to the starting value?

No. For example, 100 increased by 50% becomes 150; a 50% decrease from 150 leaves 75.

Can percentage change be negative?

Yes. A negative result indicates a decrease from the original value.
