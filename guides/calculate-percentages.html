How to Calculate Percentages: Formulas and Worked Examples

Solve common questions such as “What is 20% of 500?”, “45 is what percent of 180?” and “What is the percentage increase?”.

← Back to Tolvix tools

A percentage expresses a value as a part of 100. Percentages are used for exam marks, discounts, price changes, GST and everyday calculations. Different questions need different formulas. Check your working with the Tolvix Percentage Calculator.

1. How to find a percentage of a number

Result = Percentage ÷ 100 × Whole

Example: What is 20% of 500? Calculate 20 ÷ 100 × 500 = 100.

2. How to find what percentage one number is of another

Percentage = Part ÷ Whole × 100

Example: 45 is what percentage of 180? 45 ÷ 180 × 100 = 25%. The whole must not be zero.

3. How to calculate percentage increase or decrease

Percentage change = (New value − Original value) ÷ Original value × 100

A value rises from 80 to 100: difference = 20; 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25% increase. A fall from 100 to 80 is a 20% decrease. Always use the original value as the reference.

4. How to calculate a discount and sale price

For a ₹2,000 item with a 15% discount, savings = ₹2,000 × 15 ÷ 100 = ₹300. Sale price = ₹2,000 − ₹300 = ₹1,700, before applicable taxes or fees. Try the Discount Calculator.

5. Percentage points vs percentage change

If a rate changes from 10% to 15%, it rises by 5 percentage points. Relative to the original 10%, that is a 50% increase. These are different measures.

6. How to calculate exam percentage

If you score 420 out of 500, percentage = 420 ÷ 500 × 100 = 84%. Use the maximum marks that actually apply. If subjects have different maximum marks or your institution uses a grade-point system, follow its official calculation rules.

Common percentage mistakes

  • Dividing by the new value instead of the original value for percentage change.
  • Confusing percentage points with relative percentage increase.
  • Dividing by zero when the whole or original value is zero.
  • Assuming successive percentage changes simply add together.
  • Confusing the discount amount with the final sale price.

Frequently asked questions

What is 10% of 1,000?

10 ÷ 100 × 1,000 = 100.

How do I calculate 30 out of 40 as a percentage?

30 ÷ 40 × 100 = 75%.

How do I calculate a 20% increase?

Multiply the original value by 1.20. A 20% increase on 500 gives 600.

Can I calculate percentage change from zero?

No standard percentage-change result exists when the original value is zero because the formula divides by zero. Report the absolute change instead.
