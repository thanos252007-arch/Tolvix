How to Calculate Loan EMI in India: Formula, Example and Calculator

Learn the reducing-balance EMI formula, work through a ₹5 lakh loan example and compare monthly payments with total interest.

← Back to Tolvix tools

Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) is the regular payment made towards a loan. For a standard reducing-balance loan, each payment covers interest for that period and repays part of the outstanding principal. This guide explains the formula and how to use the Tolvix EMI Calculator.

What is the EMI formula?

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • P: principal or amount borrowed.
  • r: monthly interest rate as a decimal.
  • n: total monthly instalments.

A common monthly conversion is annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. For example, 9% annually becomes 0.0075 per month. Confirm the lender's convention and loan structure.

Example: EMI on a ₹5,00,000 loan

Assume principal ₹5,00,000, annual reducing-balance rate 9% and a five-year tenure. Monthly rate = 0.0075 and payments = 60. Estimated EMI is approximately ₹10,379 per month. Total instalments are about ₹6.23 lakh, so interest is approximately ₹1.23 lakh before fees or other charges. A lender's schedule may differ because of dates, rounding or conventions.

How to calculate EMI step by step

  1. Write down the principal in rupees.
  2. Convert the annual rate into a monthly decimal rate.
  3. Convert tenure into the number of monthly payments.
  4. Substitute the values into the reducing-balance formula.
  5. Compare the estimate with the lender's official repayment schedule.

Does a longer tenure reduce EMI?

A longer tenure usually spreads repayment across more months and can reduce the monthly EMI. But interest is charged for longer, so total interest can increase. Compare total repayment, not just the monthly instalment.

Reducing-balance vs flat interest

With reducing-balance interest, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal, which generally falls as you repay. A flat-rate quote calculates interest using the original principal for the agreed period. The same stated percentage under these methods is not directly comparable. Ask the lender which method its quote uses.

What can an EMI estimate exclude?

  • Processing and documentation fees.
  • Insurance premiums or bundled products.
  • Late-payment charges and penalties.
  • Prepayment or foreclosure conditions.
  • Floating-rate changes and reset dates.

Read the lender's Key Fact Statement and loan agreement. Compare the estimate with the official amortisation schedule before making a borrowing decision.

Frequently asked questions

Does a lower EMI always mean a cheaper loan?

No. A longer tenure can lower the monthly payment but increase total interest. Compare total repayment and fees.

Can actual EMI differ from this estimate?

Yes. Payment dates, rounding, fees, rate changes and lender conventions can affect the amount.

Is this formula suitable for every loan?

It is for a standard fixed-rate reducing-balance repayment. Flat-rate, interest-only, irregular or floating-rate products can work differently.

Does the calculator approve a loan?

No. It estimates repayments and does not assess eligibility or lender approval.
