How to Calculate Loan EMI in India: Formula and Example

An EMI estimate helps compare monthly payments, but it is not a lender quote or a substitute for reading the loan agreement.

← Back to Tolvix tools

Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) is the regular payment made towards a loan. For a standard reducing-balance loan, each instalment covers interest for that period and repays part of the principal. This guide explains the formula and how to use the Tolvix EMI Calculator.

1. EMI formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • P: loan principal (amount borrowed).
  • r: monthly interest rate as a decimal.
  • n: total number of monthly instalments.

For a quoted annual rate, a common monthly conversion is annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. For example, 9% per year becomes 0.09 ÷ 12 = 0.0075 per month. Actual lender conventions and rate structures can differ, so check the loan agreement.

2. Worked example

Assume a principal of ₹5,00,000, an annual reducing-balance rate of 9%, and a 5-year tenure. The monthly rate is 0.0075 and the number of instalments is 60. Substituting these values gives an estimated EMI of about ₹10,379 per month (rounded). Total instalments are about ₹6.23 lakh, so interest is approximately ₹1.23 lakh before fees or other charges. A lender's schedule may differ because of its exact calculation and rounding method.

4. Why a longer tenure can cost more

A longer tenure usually spreads repayment across more months, which can reduce the EMI. But interest is charged for longer, so the total interest paid can increase. Compare both the monthly payment and total repayment; do not select a loan using EMI alone.

5. Reducing-balance versus flat interest

With reducing-balance interest, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal, which generally falls as you repay. A flat-rate quote applies interest to the original principal for the agreed period and is not directly comparable to the same percentage reducing-balance rate. Ask the lender which method the quote uses before comparing offers.

6. Costs a basic EMI estimate may exclude

  • Processing or documentation fees.
  • Insurance premiums or bundled products.
  • Late-payment charges and penalties.
  • Prepayment or foreclosure conditions.
  • Floating-rate changes and reset dates.

Read the Key Fact Statement and loan agreement, and compare the lender's official amortisation schedule with your estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Does a lower EMI always mean a cheaper loan?

No. A longer tenure can lower the monthly EMI but increase total interest. Compare total repayment and fees.

Can the actual EMI differ from this estimate?

Yes. Lender conventions, dates, rounding, fees, rate changes and repayment structure can affect the amount.

Is the EMI formula the same for every loan?

The formula shown is for a standard fixed-rate reducing-balance repayment. Interest-only, flat-rate, irregular or floating-rate products can work differently.

Does the calculator approve a loan?

No. It estimates repayments from the values entered and does not check eligibility, credit score or lender approval.
