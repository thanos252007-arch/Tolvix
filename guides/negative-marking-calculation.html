How to Calculate Negative Marking in Exams: Formula and Examples

Find your net exam score when correct answers earn marks and wrong answers lose marks, with clear examples for common marking schemes.

← Back to Tolvix tools

To calculate a score under negative marking, add the marks earned for correct answers and subtract the penalty for incorrect answers. The deduction depends on the exam's instructions. Use the Tolvix Negative Marking Calculator after checking the marking scheme on your question paper or official exam notice.

What is the negative marking formula?

Net score = (Correct answers × Marks per correct answer) − (Wrong answers × Penalty per wrong answer)

Questions left unanswered often receive zero, but this is not universal. Some exams use different rules for unanswered, multiple-selected or partially correct responses. Confirm the instructions for your specific test.

Example 1: +4 for correct and −1 for wrong

Suppose a test awards 4 marks for a correct answer, deducts 1 mark for a wrong answer and gives zero for an unanswered question. A student has 50 correct, 10 wrong and 5 unanswered.

  • Correct marks = 50 × 4 = 200.
  • Negative marks = 10 × 1 = 10.
  • Unanswered marks = 5 × 0 = 0.
  • Net score = 200 − 10 = 190 marks.

Example 2: one-third negative marking

If a question carries 3 marks and the penalty is one-third of the question's marks, one wrong answer deducts 3 ÷ 3 = 1 mark. With 20 correct and 6 wrong answers, the score is (20 × 3) − (6 × 1) = 54 marks, assuming unanswered questions receive zero.

How to calculate negative marking step by step

  1. Find the marks awarded for one correct answer.
  2. Find the penalty for one wrong answer. It may be fixed or a fraction of the question's marks.
  3. Count correct, incorrect and unanswered questions separately.
  4. Multiply correct answers by the positive marks.
  5. Multiply wrong answers by the penalty.
  6. Subtract the penalty total from the positive marks total.

For a quick check, enter the marking values and answer counts in the Negative Marking Calculator. Enter the penalty as a deduction per wrong answer, not as a positive bonus.

How do I calculate one-fourth or one-third negative marking?

Divide the marks for a correct answer by 4 for a one-fourth penalty, or by 3 for a one-third penalty. If a correct answer is worth 4 marks, a one-fourth deduction is 1 mark. If it is worth 3 marks, a one-third deduction is 1 mark. Follow the exam's rounding instructions where relevant.

Negative marks vs accuracy percentage

Attempted-question accuracy = Correct answers ÷ (Correct answers + Wrong answers) × 100. This excludes unanswered questions. Net score also depends on the positive marks and penalty values, so accuracy percentage alone does not determine the final score.

Common mistakes

  • Deducting marks for unanswered questions when the rules say zero.
  • Using one-fourth instead of one-third, or the wrong penalty value.
  • Entering total attempts as the number of wrong answers.
  • Assuming all questions carry the same marks when they do not.
  • Assuming a negative total is automatically capped at zero.

Frequently asked questions

How much is one-fourth negative marking?

It is one-fourth of the marks assigned to a correct answer. If a correct answer earns 4 marks, one wrong answer deducts 1 mark.

Do unanswered questions have negative marks?

Often they receive zero, but check the instructions for the particular exam.

Can my score become negative?

The arithmetic can produce a negative value. Whether the official score is retained as negative or capped depends on the exam rules.

Is the Tolvix result an official exam score?

No. It estimates a score from the values entered. The exam authority's marking scheme and result take precedence.
