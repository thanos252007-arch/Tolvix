How to Calculate GST in India: Formula, Examples and Online Calculator

Learn the difference between a price before GST and a price that already includes GST, with simple examples in rupees.

← Back to Tolvix tools

To calculate Goods and Services Tax (GST), first identify whether the price is before tax or already includes tax. The formulas are different. This guide explains the arithmetic with Indian rupee examples and shows how to check a result using the Tolvix GST Calculator.

1. Add GST to a base price

Use this method when the quoted amount does not yet include GST.

GST amount = Base price × GST rate ÷ 100
Price including GST = Base price + GST amount

Example: add 18% GST to ₹1,000

  1. GST = ₹1,000 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹180.
  2. Total = ₹1,000 + ₹180 = ₹1,180.

2. Remove GST from an inclusive price

If the displayed amount already includes GST, do not calculate the tax as a simple percentage of the total. Recover the base amount using the rate factor.

Base price = Inclusive price × 100 ÷ (100 + GST rate)
GST component = Inclusive price − Base price

Example: extract 18% GST from ₹1,180

Base price = ₹1,180 × 100 ÷ 118 = ₹1,000. GST component = ₹1,180 − ₹1,000 = ₹180.

3. Understand common GST rates

GST rates are not universal. Different goods and services can have different rates, exemptions or special treatments, and the applicable classification matters. Do not assume that 5%, 12%, 18% or 28% applies to a particular item without checking current official guidance. The calculator performs the percentage arithmetic for the rate you enter; it does not identify the correct legal rate.

4. CGST, SGST and IGST

For many intra-state supplies, the tax is split between CGST and SGST (or UTGST). For many inter-state supplies, IGST applies. For example, if a confirmed combined rate is 18% on a ₹1,000 taxable value for an eligible intra-state supply, the arithmetic split is 9% CGST (₹90) and 9% SGST (₹90). The place of supply and transaction rules determine the correct treatment; this simple calculator does not make that decision.

6. Common mistakes to avoid

  • Taxing an inclusive total as if it were a base price: use the extraction formula for inclusive amounts.
  • Assuming one rate fits everything: verify the rate and classification.
  • Confusing the tax split: CGST/SGST versus IGST depends on the supply, not simply the calculator input.
  • Ignoring discounts or special treatment: the taxable value can depend on transaction details.
  • Rounding too early: retain precision during the calculation and follow applicable invoice rules.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate 18% GST on ₹2,500?

₹2,500 × 18 ÷ 100 = ₹450 GST. The total before any other adjustments is ₹2,950.

How do I find GST included in ₹2,950 at 18%?

Base = ₹2,950 × 100 ÷ 118 = ₹2,500. GST included = ₹450.

Is 18% GST charged on every product?

No. The applicable rate depends on the goods or service and current tax rules. Verify the classification and rate for the specific supply.

Does this calculator create a GST invoice?

No. It is an arithmetic aid. It does not validate GSTINs, classify supplies, determine place of supply or create a compliant tax invoice.
