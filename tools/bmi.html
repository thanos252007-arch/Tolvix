What BMI means

Body Mass Index is calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared. It is a screening measurement used in adult population settings, not a direct measurement of body fat or a diagnosis. The same BMI value can mean different things for people with different body compositions and health backgrounds.

Important limitations

  • Adult BMI categories are not intended for children and teenagers; age- and sex-specific growth charts are used for them.
  • BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat or show where body fat is carried.
  • Pregnancy, fluid changes, athletic build and some medical conditions can affect interpretation.

Use the result as a rough reference only. For an individual health assessment, discuss it with a qualified healthcare professional who can consider your wider health context.

Worked example: calculate adult BMI

Suppose an adult weighs 70 kg and is 175 cm tall. Convert the height to metres (1.75), square it (1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625), then divide weight by that value: 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.86. The result is a BMI of approximately 22.9.

Use consistent units: this calculator expects kilograms and centimetres. Entering 1.75 in the centimetre field would produce an incorrect result.

BMI calculator FAQs

What is the BMI formula?

BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in metres × height in metres).

Can I use adult BMI for a child?

No. Children and teenagers require age- and sex-specific growth-chart interpretation. This page is intended for adults.

Does BMI measure body fat directly?

No. BMI is a screening measure based on height and weight. It does not distinguish muscle from fat or diagnose a health condition.
