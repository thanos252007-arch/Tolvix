2D vector components and magnitude

Resolve a two-dimensional vector into horizontal and vertical components. Enter magnitude and direction to see how much of the vector acts along each axis. This is useful for forces, velocity, displacement and other vector quantities.

Formula and variables

For magnitude A and angle θ from the positive x-axis: Ax = A cos(θ), Ay = A sin(θ). Magnitude is √(Ax² + Ay²), and direction can be recovered with atan2(Ay, Ax).

Worked example

A 10-unit vector at 30° has x-component about 8.66 and y-component 5.00. The components combine to reproduce the original magnitude of 10.

Assumptions and limitations

Check the axis from which the angle is measured and its direction. Components can be negative when a vector points left or downward. This is a two-dimensional Cartesian model.

Frequently asked questions

What formula is used?

For magnitude A and angle θ from the positive x-axis: Ax = A cos(θ), Ay = A sin(θ). Magnitude is √(Ax² + Ay²), and direction can be recovered with atan2(Ay, Ax).

Are these results exact in real life?

They are results from the stated idealised model. Real measurements can differ because of friction, drag, measurement uncertainty and other effects described above.

Which units should I use?

Use the SI units indicated beside the calculator inputs. Convert quantities before entering them if they use different units.
