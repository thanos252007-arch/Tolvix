Work, kinetic energy and power

Calculate mechanical work by a constant force, kinetic energy from mass and speed, or average power from work and elapsed time. These quantities describe energy transfer and how quickly work is performed.

Formula and variables

For a constant force at angle θ to displacement, W = Fd cos(θ). Kinetic energy is KE = ½mv². Average power is P = W/t. Work and energy use joules; power uses watts.

Worked example

A 10 N force acting along a 3 m displacement does 30 J of work. If that work takes 2 seconds, average power is 15 W.

Assumptions and limitations

Work is zero when force is perpendicular to displacement and negative when it opposes motion. The kinetic-energy formula gives translational kinetic energy, not rotational energy or total energy.
