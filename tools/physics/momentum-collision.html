Momentum and perfectly inelastic collisions

Estimate shared velocity after two objects collide and move together. This calculator uses conservation of linear momentum for a perfectly inelastic collision, a useful ideal model for introductory mechanics.

Formula and variables

For two objects that stick together, m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v. Thus v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂)/(m₁ + m₂), with signed velocities using a consistent positive direction.

Worked example

A 2 kg cart moving at 3 m/s catches a stationary 1 kg cart and they stick together. Their shared velocity is 2 m/s.

Assumptions and limitations

Momentum is conserved when net external impulse is negligible. Kinetic energy is generally not conserved in a perfectly inelastic collision; some becomes deformation, sound and heat. Use signed velocities for opposite directions.

Frequently asked questions

What formula is used?

For two objects that stick together, m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)v. Thus v = (m₁u₁ + m₂u₂)/(m₁ + m₂), with signed velocities using a consistent positive direction.

Are these results exact in real life?

They are results from the stated idealised model. Real measurements can differ because of friction, drag, measurement uncertainty and other effects described above.
