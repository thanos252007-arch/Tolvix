Ohm's law voltage, current and resistance

Calculate voltage, current or resistance for a simple resistive circuit. The calculator also connects electrical power with voltage and current, helping students practise basic circuit relationships.

Formula and variables

Ohm's law is V = IR. Electrical power can be calculated using P = VI, P = I²R or P = V²/R. Voltage is measured in volts, current in amperes, resistance in ohms and power in watts.

Worked example

A 12 V supply across a 6 Ω resistor produces 2 A and dissipates 24 W under the ideal resistive model.

Assumptions and limitations

Ohm's law describes ohmic components under conditions where resistance is approximately constant. Real components can heat up, change resistance or exceed their ratings. Do not use calculated values as a substitute for safe circuit design.
