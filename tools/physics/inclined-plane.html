Inclined plane forces and friction

Explore the forces on a block along a sloping surface. This calculator estimates normal reaction and acceleration using incline angle, gravitational acceleration and friction coefficient, connecting free-body diagrams with Newton's laws.

Formula and variables

For a block on an incline, the downslope weight component is mg sin(θ), while normal force is mg cos(θ) when no other perpendicular forces act. Kinetic friction has magnitude μN and opposes sliding.

Worked example

For a 2 kg block on a 30° incline with μ = 0.10, the downslope weight component is about 9.81 N and normal force about 16.99 N. Kinetic friction is about 1.70 N.

Assumptions and limitations

The model assumes a rigid straight incline, constant kinetic-friction coefficient and no additional applied forces. Static friction can prevent motion; it is not interchangeable with kinetic friction. Confirm the block is sliding before applying this model.

Frequently asked questions

What formula is used?

For a block on an incline, the downslope weight component is mg sin(θ), while normal force is mg cos(θ) when no other perpendicular forces act. Kinetic friction has magnitude μN and opposes sliding.

Are these results exact in real life?

They are results from the stated idealised model. Real measurements can differ because of friction, drag, measurement uncertainty and other effects described above.
