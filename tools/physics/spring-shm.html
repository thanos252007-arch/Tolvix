Spring oscillation period and frequency

Estimate the natural oscillation period and frequency of an ideal mass attached to a spring. This simple harmonic motion model applies when the spring obeys Hooke's law and damping is negligible.

Formula and variables

For a mass-spring system, angular frequency ω = √(k/m), period T = 2π√(m/k), and frequency f = 1/T. Here k is spring stiffness in N/m and m is mass in kg.

Worked example

A 0.50 kg mass attached to a spring with k = 200 N/m has a period of about 0.314 s and frequency about 3.18 Hz.

Assumptions and limitations

The result assumes a linear spring, small oscillations, negligible damping and a mass much larger than the spring's effective mass. Real systems lose energy through friction and air resistance.

Frequently asked questions

What formula is used?

For a mass-spring system, angular frequency ω = √(k/m), period T = 2π√(m/k), and frequency f = 1/T. Here k is spring stiffness in N/m and m is mass in kg.

Are these results exact in real life?

They are results from the stated idealised model. Real measurements can differ because of friction, drag, measurement uncertainty and other effects described above.
