Projectile motion: range, flight time and maximum height

A projectile launched at an angle follows a curved path when gravity is the only acceleration. Enter launch speed and angle to estimate horizontal range, total flight time and peak height. The model assumes launch and landing at the same elevation, constant gravitational acceleration and no air resistance.

Formula and variables

For launch speed u and angle θ: horizontal range R = u² sin(2θ) / g; flight time T = 2u sin(θ) / g; maximum height H = u² sin²(θ) / (2g). Horizontal and vertical motion are treated independently.

Worked example

With u = 20 m/s, θ = 30° and g = 9.81 m/s², the idealised range is about 35.3 m, flight time 2.04 s and maximum height 5.10 m.

Assumptions and limitations

This model ignores air resistance, wind, spin and changes in elevation between launch and landing. Use consistent units and treat the result as an ideal physics estimate, not a real-world trajectory prediction.

Frequently asked questions

What formula is used?

For launch speed u and angle θ: horizontal range R = u² sin(2θ) / g; flight time T = 2u sin(θ) / g; maximum height H = u² sin²(θ) / (2g). Horizontal and vertical motion are treated independently.

Are these results exact in real life?

They are results from the stated idealised model. Real measurements can differ because of friction, drag, measurement uncertainty and other effects described above.
